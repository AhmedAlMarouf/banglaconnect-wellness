A new community health initiative

Diabetes care that speaks your language — and understands your kitchen.

Bangladeshi immigrants in Canada face some of the highest risks of type 2 diabetes — yet most prevention resources aren't written for our food, our language, or our life here. BanglaConnect Wellness is a volunteer-run effort to close that gap, one conversation at a time.

Join the founding community Why we started

Why this matters

Type 2 diabetes doesn't affect every community the same way — and the way Canada talks about it doesn't reach every community the same way either.

Risk starts earlier

South Asians, including Bangladeshi Canadians, tend to develop type 2 diabetes at a younger age and a lower body weight than the general population — often before the warning signs Canadian health guidance is built around.

Advice rarely fits

Most nutrition and diabetes education here assumes a different diet, a different vocabulary, and different assumptions about food and family life than a Bangladeshi household's daily reality.

Silence has a cost

Diabetes still carries quiet stigma in many Bangladeshi households. People often manage it alone rather than ask their family, mosque, or community for support.

What we're building

We're starting small and volunteer-run — four pillars, built in order, each one tested with the community before we scale it up.

  1. 1

    Bangla-language workshops

    Plain-language sessions on food, blood sugar, and daily routines — taught in Bangla and English, by people who understand the kitchen you're cooking in.

  2. 2

    Community screening days

    Partnering with local clinics and community centres to bring blood-sugar screening to places people already gather, instead of asking them to seek it out.

  3. 3

    Peer support circles

    Small groups led by community members living with diabetes, for the kind of honest conversation a pamphlet can't have.

  4. 4

    A living resource hub

    Recipes, glossaries, and guidance that translate Canadian nutrition advice into the ingredients and dishes of a Bangladeshi kitchen.

"I'm starting BanglaConnect Wellness as a one-person, volunteer effort — because I kept hearing the same story from Bangladeshi families in Canada: they knew something was wrong, but nowhere felt like it was speaking to them. This is the beginning, not the whole answer."

— Founder, BanglaConnect Wellness
